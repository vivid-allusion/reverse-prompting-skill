# Reading one still into its shot file

Open the still at full size and fill its `SHOT_NNN.md`.

| Section | Write |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Wide, medium, close-up, insert; the angle |
| **Hero** | Who or what the shot is about, in one line: [[hero]] |
| **In frame** | A literal inventory: the people, what each does and holds, the place, the light sources that show |
| **Out of frame, by effect** | What matters but cannot be in this frame: a fire seen only as its light, a person spoken to, a street heard |
| **Elements** | The table of elements with a role: [[elements]] |
| **Notes** | Where the still disagrees with the story, the canon or the old motion line, and what you changed |

## The still is a storyboard, not a look

Keep its content, its place in the film and its shot size. Change:

- **The look.** The default grade, glow, haze everywhere, over-sharp skin: gone. The project's look comes from its own templates.
- **Centred hero framing:** re-blocked off-centre.
- **Light sources in frame** that are there as decoration, candles on every table or lamps in a row: moved out of frame and written as their effect.
- **The old motion line,** almost always a slow push-in or dolly: rewritten as what a handheld operator does.
- **Text in frame:** signs, labels and hull names become blank or unreadable.

## Literal, not situational

"He sits by the fire" names a fire; "close-up of his face, warm flicker from below frame-left; the fire is never in frame" describes a frame. Write the second. The method is the `cinematic-realism` skill.

## When the still and the story disagree

The story wins on what happens and who is there; the canon sheet wins on how someone looks; the still wins on shot size and composition. Write the call in the notes and move on: [[decisions]].
